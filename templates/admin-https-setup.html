🔒 HTTPS Setup

Offline features (browsing your inventory and logging range sessions with zero signal) need a secure connection to work at all — plain "not secure" browsing silently disables them. This server already has one, generated locally with no internet involved. Each phone/tablet just needs a one-time, one-tap step to trust it.

Step 1 — On the phone, scan this or visit the link

Make sure the phone is on the same WiFi network as this server, then either:

  • Scan the QR code with the phone's camera, or
  • Open this address in the phone's browser:

This downloads one small file — the certificate that proves this specific server is really this server, nothing more.

Step 2 — Install it on the phone

Android

  1. Don't tap the downloaded file directly — Android blocks installing a CA certificate that way. Instead, search Settings for "certificate" and choose Install a certificate
  2. Choose CA certificate specifically (not "VPN & app user certificate")
  3. Pick the downloaded file from Downloads. You'll see a warning here — see "Why does my phone warn me about this?" below, it's expected and explained there
  4. If it still shows "not secure" afterward, fully force-stop Chrome (Settings → Apps → Chrome → Force stop) and reopen it — Chrome can hang onto a stale "untrusted" result from before you installed the certificate

iPhone / iPad

  1. Open the downloaded profile — it'll prompt to install
  2. Settings → General → VPN & Device Management → install it
  3. Then: Settings → General → About → Certificate Trust Settings → turn on full trust for it (easy to miss — it won't work without this last step)

❓ Why does my phone warn me about this?

Your phone will show something like "A third party or your organization can monitor your online activity, including emails, apps, and secure websites." That's a generic warning Android/iOS shows for trusting any certificate authority — it's not specific to this app, and it's not wrong to take seriously. Here's exactly what this does and doesn't do:

  • What it actually does: lets your phone verify "yes, this is genuinely my own server" instead of showing a security warning every time you open the app. That's the only thing it does.
  • What it doesn't do: it isn't spyware, isn't a VPN profile, doesn't route your traffic anywhere, and gives this app — or us — no new ability to see your phone's data, your browsing on other sites, your location, or anything else. No code runs on your phone because of this; it's a cryptographic trust decision, nothing more.
  • Where the actual key lives: the private key that makes this certificate work exists only on this server, generated here, never uploaded anywhere, never touching your phone. Only someone with physical or remote access to this specific machine could ever misuse it — and if that ever happened, it'd be a compromise of your own server, not something this feature itself enables.
  • Your phone already trusts roughly 100 certificate authorities out of the box (Let's Encrypt, DigiCert, and so on) without ever asking you. This is the same kind of trust decision, scoped to one more authority — specifically, your own.

Step 3 — Confirm it worked

Reload this app on the phone. The address bar should show a lock icon with no warning. Repeat this whole process once per device — it's a one-time setup per phone/tablet, not something you need to redo later.
